## 4. Pressure-Test Prompt & Output

### Prompt used:

```
This is my proof statement:

"I build multi-agent AI systems that ship to production — not single-model
wrappers, but coordinated backend architectures where multiple autonomous
agents divide real work and communicate with each other. I am proving this
to Mpho Raborife, Engineering Lead at FlyRank, so he will email me directly
to discuss a contract engagement on an AI engineering project."

This is my sitemap:
- Hero: claim headline, "6 agents in production" + "4 full-stack multi-agent
  systems" stats, Email Me (primary) + View Case Studies (secondary) CTAs
- About: first-person restatement naming all 4 systems, ends with email CTA box
- Case Studies: 4 cards in 2×2 grid (PulsePM AI / 6 agents / Dubai hackathon,
  ForgeMind OS / agent OS / OpenAI Build Week, ForgeMind AI / 5 agents / AMD
  hackathon, FinPilot AI / LLM finance / Pinch hackathon). Inline stack chips
  below (Python, FastAPI, Multi-Agent Orchestration, LLM Integration,
  PostgreSQL, Docker).
- Contact: "Let's talk specifics." + email as dominant CTA + GitHub/LinkedIn
  as quiet secondary links.

Does this sitemap walk my one person from landing, to believing me, to taking
my one action? Tell me which pages earn their place, which are wasted, and
what is missing. Be specific, not encouraging.
```

### Key feedback received:

The pressure-test identified that the case study cards lack **outcome signals** — they say "Built for X hackathon" but not whether they placed, won, or were evaluated. An engineering lead cares about external validation, not just participation. The cards should note results where available (e.g., "Dubai AI Week finalist" or "competing July 2026") to distinguish shipped-and-validated from shipped-and-untested.

### Change I will make:

Add hackathon outcomes or current competition status to each case study card. Where results exist, state them plainly. Where competitions are ongoing (FinPilot/Pinch, ForgeMind AI/AMD), mark "Competing" instead of defaulting to "Active" — honesty about status is more credible than assuming everything shipped.
